\begin{abstract}
	These notes develop the mathematics required for the first year of an economics
	doctoral program, and somewhat beyond it. They follow the topics of the
	Mathematics Review Course taught at the Study Center Gerzensee within the Swiss
	Program for Beginning Doctoral Students in Economics --- probability and
	conditional expectation, single- and multi-variable calculus, static
	optimisation, real analysis, and discrete-time dynamic programming --- and
	extend each of them to the level of rigor at which the results are actually used
	in microeconomic theory, macroeconomics, and econometrics.

	Every definition carries its standing hypotheses, every theorem states the
	structure required of the underlying space, and proofs are given in full except
	where a result is quoted from a source cited at the point of use. Economic
	applications are worked rather than gestured at: the environment, the
	correspondence between mathematical and economic objects, the derivation, and
	the consequences of relaxing the hypotheses are all stated.
\end{abstract}
